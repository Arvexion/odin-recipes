Description

Traditional chocolate fudge relies on boiling sugar, butter, and milk to a precise "soft-ball" stage, then letting it cool undisturbed. This careful temperature control ensures that microscopic sugar crystals form during the final stirring process, giving the fudge its iconic, ultra-creamy, melt-in-your-mouth texture.

For a quick and foolproof alternative, a popular shortcut recipe combines semi-sweet chocolate chips with sweetened condensed milk and butter. By melting these ingredients together over low heat, you completely bypass the risky boiling process while still achieving a rich, dense, and deliciously fudgy square.

Ingredients

  • Semi-sweet chocolate chips: 3 cups (or 18 ounces)
  • Sweetened condensed milk: 1 can (14 ounces)
  • Unsalted butter: 2 tablespoons
  • Vanilla extract: 1 teaspoon
  • Fine sea salt: ¼ teaspoon

Steps

  1. Line an 8x8 inch square baking pan with parchment paper, leaving a slight overhang on the sides to easily lift the fudge out later.
  2. Combine the chocolate chips, sweetened condensed milk, and unsalted butter in a medium, heavy-bottomed saucepan over low heat.
  3. Stir the mixture constantly with a rubber spatula until the chocolate chips are completely melted and the mixture is entirely smooth and glossy.
  4. Remove the saucepan from the heat source immediately to prevent the chocolate from scorching.
  5. Stir in the vanilla extract and sea salt until fully incorporated into the thick chocolate mixture.
  6. Scrape the hot fudge into the prepared baking pan, spreading it evenly and smoothing the top surface with your spatula.
  7. Place the pan in the refrigerator for at least 2 hours, or until the fudge is completely firm and set.
  8. Lift the fudge out of the pan using the parchment paper overhang, place it on a cutting board, and use a sharp knife to slice it into small, rich squares.

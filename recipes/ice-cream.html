Description

Traditional chocolate ice cream is made by crafting a custard base using milk, heavy cream, sugar, and egg yolks, which are gently cooked until thick. Unsweetened cocoa powder or melted dark chocolate is whisked into this warm mixture to provide a deep flavor, which is then thoroughly chilled before being poured into an ice cream maker to churn and freeze.

For an incredibly easy shortcut, a no-churn recipe completely skips the eggs, cooking, and churning machine by using whipped cream instead. You simply fold a mixture of cocoa powder and sweetened condensed milk into stiffly whipped heavy cream, which locks in thousands of tiny air bubbles to keep the ice cream soft and scoopable once frozen.

Ingredients

  • Heavy whipping cream: 2 cups (cold)
  • Sweetened condensed milk: 1 can (14 ounces, chilled)
  • Unsweetened cocoa powder: ½ cup
  • Vanilla extract: 1 teaspoon
  • Fine sea salt: A tiny pinch

Steps

  1. Place a metal loaf pan or an 8x8 inch baking pan into the freezer to chill while you prepare the mixture.
  2. In a medium bowl, whisk together the sweetened condensed milk, unsweetened cocoa powder, vanilla extract, and sea salt until completely smooth and free of lumps.
  3. In a separate large bowl, pour in the cold heavy whipping cream and beat it using a hand mixer or stand mixer until stiff peaks form.
  4. Add a large scoop of the whipped cream to the chocolate mixture and stir it in to lighten up the thick condensed milk.
  5. Pour the remaining chocolate mixture over the rest of the whipped cream and gently fold them together with a rubber spatula just until combined, being careful not to deflate the whipped cream.
  6. Pour the remaining chocolate mixture over the rest of the whipped cream and gently fold them together with a rubber spatula just until combined, being careful not to deflate the whipped cream.
  7. Cover the pan tightly with plastic wrap or aluminum foil to prevent ice crystals from forming.
  8. Freeze for at least 6 hours, or overnight, until completely firm and ready to scoop.
